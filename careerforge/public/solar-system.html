Real-time WebGL

Eight worlds, one moving sky.

A living model of the solar system running at full frame rate in your browser. Every surface here is generated from noise at load time — no textures, no photographs, nothing downloaded but the code itself.

The planets

Pick a world. The camera will chase it.

Click any planet, label or chip and the view locks on and follows it along its orbit. Drag anywhere to swing the camera around. Orbital periods are held in true ratio to one another, gently compressed so the outer giants still move while you watch.

How it works

Everything you see is computed, not fetched.

01

Procedural surfaces

Each planet's colour and bump map is drawn to a canvas at load using 3D value noise sampled on the sphere, so the bands and continents wrap seamlessly with no visible seam.

02

One light, real falloff

A single point light sits inside the Sun. Neptune is genuinely dim because it is genuinely far away — the terminator you see on each globe is the actual lit hemisphere.

03

Honest orbital ratios

Mercury laps the Sun roughly four times for each of Earth's circuits. Radii are compressed for framing; the timing between worlds is not.

04

Bloom, belt and rings

An unreal bloom pass gives the corona its bite, twelve hundred rocks fill the asteroid belt, and Saturn's rings carry their own Cassini division.

Scale

The numbers that make it hard to draw.

If the Sun were the size it appears here, Neptune's true orbit would sit several kilometres off the edge of your screen. This model trades distance for legibility and keeps everything else.
